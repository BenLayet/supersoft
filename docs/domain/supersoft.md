# Supersoft in brief

Supersoft helps a **maker** build and maintain a web or mobile application for a **customer**, while keeping that customer part of the conversation from the first sketch to the running application.

It exists because the expensive failure in this work is not writing code — it is building the wrong thing, slowly, and finding out late.

Supersoft answers that with one commitment: **the specification is the source of truth, it is written in the customer's own words, and everything else is a consequence of it.**

What it covers:

- the **[specification](specification.md)** — what the application must do and be, described so that the people who commissioned it can read it, correct it and recognise their own business in it. It has three parts: the domain, the [brand](brand.md), and the [stories](stories.md);
- the **[conversation](conversation.md)** — remarks, questions, change requests and agreements, each attached to a precise part of the specification, so that no feedback is lost and nothing is agreed by silence;
- the **[prototype and demonstration](prototype.md)** — a runnable application, filled with plausible fictional data, that can be clicked through and discussed long before anything real is built;
- **[building by the customer](customer-building.md)** — customers who want to change things themselves, doing so by changing the specification rather than the application;
- **[real use and handover](delivery.md)** — putting the application in front of real people, keeping the specification true afterwards, and guaranteeing the customer can leave with everything at any moment;
- the **[shared patterns](shared-patterns.md)** — descriptions of business that recur from one project to the next, held in common so that no one starts from nothing.

## Who it serves

Supersoft is built first for makers working directly with their customers, on small teams or alone.

Its longer purpose is to lower the cost of good software for organisations that cannot afford it — charities, associations, and people meeting real needs with no budget. Those organisations have needs that repeat: members, events, donations, volunteers, people to follow up on. Every such need described once and held in common ([shared patterns](shared-patterns.md)) is a need the next organisation does not have to pay to describe again.

This purpose is not decoration. It is why [handover](delivery.md) is a guarantee rather than a feature, why [accessibility](brand.md) is part of every brand rather than an option, and why patterns are common property.
