# Conversation and agreement

Everything a customer and a maker say to each other about the application belongs to the project, attached to the part of the [specification](specification.md) it concerns. A decision taken in a conversation that is not written next to what it decides will be lost, and will be re-decided differently.

## Remark

A **remark** is anything a participant says about a precise element: a statement in the specification, a [story](stories.md), a [brand](brand.md) choice, or something seen on a [prototype](prototype.md) screen.

A remark is always attached to something. "The colours feel cold" attached to the brand is useful; the same sentence in a message on its own is not.

**Rule.** A remark is never simply closed. It ends in one of three ways: it changes the specification, it is refused with a stated reason, or it becomes an [open point](#open-point) still waiting for someone. Silently dropping a remark is the failure this whole part of the product exists to prevent.

## Question

A **question** is a remark that needs an answer from a named person before the work can go on. It names who must answer.

A question standing on a statement prevents that statement from becoming **agreed**.

## Change request

A **change request** is a proposed modification to the specification, stated precisely enough to be accepted as written. "Members should be able to cancel up to 48 hours before" is a change request; "cancellation is too strict" is a remark that has not become one yet.

Turning remarks into change requests is most of the maker's craft. It is where a vague dissatisfaction becomes a decidable question.

## Agreement

**Agreement** is the customer stating that a part of the specification reflects what they want.

- It names **who gave it**, on a **named element**, in a **precise version of that element**, on a **date**.
- It is never implied by silence, by a passing "looks good", or by time going by.
- It can be **withdrawn**. A customer discovering on a [prototype](prototype.md) that what they agreed to is not what they meant is the system working, not a failure.

Nobody is prevented from agreeing, and no title is checked. An agreement is worth what its author's word is worth, which is why the record always says who gave it: what settles a statement is that the people the application is for have said yes. A maker approving their own sentence has recorded something true about themselves and nothing about the business.

An agreement covers the version it was given on, and nothing else. When the element changes, the new version simply carries no agreement, and is proposed again like any other version never agreed. Nothing needs to expire: agreeing is agreeing to a text, not to a subject.

## Open point

An **open point** is anything the project knows it does not know: an unanswered question, a statement marked **to confirm**, a **questioned** statement, or a remark not yet resolved.

The open points of a project are always countable and always visible. A project's honesty is measured by whether its list of open points is shorter than the truth.

**Rule.** Going into [real use](delivery.md) with open points is allowed — it is normal. Going into real use without knowing what they are is not.
