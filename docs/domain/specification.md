# Specification

The **specification** describes what the application must do. It is written in the words of the people who commissioned it, so that they can read it, correct it, and recognise their own business in it.

It has three parts, which grow together rather than in sequence.

## Discovery

A **question** is something the project knows it does not know. It is written down as soon as it appears, it names nobody in particular, and it stays visible until it is answered.

**Rule.** The open questions of a project are always countable. Building with open questions is normal; not knowing what they are is not.

## The domain

A **term** is one concept of the business, with one name and one definition, in the customer's words. The same name is then used everywhere: in the specification, in the stories, on the screens and in the code.

A **rule** is one thing that is true of the business, written as one sentence a customer can confirm or deny.

**Rule.** A rule not written in the specification does not exist. It will not be built, and nobody is at fault when it is missing.

**Rule.** A rule says what the business requires, never what the application is made of.

## The solution

The solution is what the application does about that domain. It is described as [stories](stories.md) and nothing else: a solution that cannot be told as things people do with it has not been understood yet.

## Agreement

A rule is **proposed** when it is written, and **agreed** once the customer has confirmed it.

**Rule.** Rewriting an agreed rule makes it proposed again. Agreement is given to a sentence, not to a subject.
