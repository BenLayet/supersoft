# Brand and style

The **brand** is the part of the [specification](specification.md) that says what the application should feel like: who it speaks to, how it speaks, and how it looks.

It is written down for the same reason the domain is: so that it is decided once, by the customer, rather than re-invented screen by screen.

## What it holds

- **Identity** — the name of the application, what it is for, and who it is for. One or two sentences, in the customer's words.
- **Tone of voice** — how the application addresses people: formal or familiar, warm or neutral, sparing or explanatory. Includes what it must never sound like, which is often easier for a customer to state than what it should.
- **Vocabulary** — the words the interface uses for the customer's own concepts. Taken from the domain, never invented by a screen.
- **Colours** — a small set, each with a stated purpose (the main colour, the colour of a warning, the colour of a destructive action), rather than a palette without meaning.
- **Typography** — the typefaces and the sizes, expressed as a scale.
- **Density and spacing** — how much breathing room the application has; whether it favours showing a lot at once or showing little clearly.
- **Imagery and motion** — what kind of pictures the application uses, if any, and how much it moves.

## Accessibility

Every brand states its **accessibility commitments**, and they are part of the brand rather than a separate concern to be added later:

- text is legible: sufficient contrast against its background, and resizable without breaking the screen;
- every action can be reached without a mouse;
- every action can be understood without seeing it;
- colour is never the only way information is conveyed;
- movement can be turned off.

**Rule.** A brand choice that breaks an accessibility commitment is not accepted. When they conflict, the commitment wins and the brand choice is changed. The people Supersoft exists for are disproportionately the people that inaccessible software excludes.

## The rules that matter

**A screen never invents its own style.** Everything visible comes from the brand. A screen that needs something the brand does not have is a signal that the brand is incomplete — the brand is extended, and the change applies everywhere at once.

**The brand is decided once and applies to the whole application**, including the parts the customer never sees, such as administration screens. Consistency there is not vanity: it is what makes the application learnable.
