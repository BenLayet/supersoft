# Prototype and demonstration

## Prototype

A **prototype** is a runnable version of the application, produced from the [specification](specification.md), complete enough to be clicked through and deliberately incomplete everywhere else.

Its purpose is to make the specification visible. People cannot correct a document about their own business nearly as well as they can correct a screen showing it. Most of what a specification gets wrong is discovered here.

**Rule.** A prototype runs **without any outside service**. Nothing it does depends on a payment being taken, a message being sent, a video being hosted or anyone being billed. This is what lets it be shown anywhere, at any moment, by anyone, at no cost and with no consequence.

**Rule.** A prototype is **disposable**. It can always be rebuilt from the specification. No decision, no rule and no wording lives only in a prototype — if something is true only in the prototype, it is not yet true at all.

## Fictional data

A prototype is filled with **fictional data**: invented people, events, contents and histories, chosen to be plausible.

Plausible matters more than it sounds. A prototype showing three members named "Test User" tests nothing; a prototype showing two hundred members with realistic names, a handful of awkward cases and one person whose membership lapsed mid-year provokes the remarks that matter.

Fictional data includes the **awkward cases** on purpose: the longest name, the empty list, the person who belongs to nothing, the event that is full.

**Rule.** Real personal data never appears in a prototype. Not a real member list, not a real donor list, not exported and anonymised — invented.

## Scenario

A **scenario** is a prepared path through the prototype, with the fictional data arranged so that a particular situation can be shown: a member whose membership is about to lapse, an event with one place left.

Scenarios exist so that a demonstration can show a rule rather than describe it.

## Demonstration

A **demonstration** is a session in which the prototype is shown to the customer and discussed.

Remarks made during a demonstration attach to what was on the screen when they were made. A demonstration that produces no [remarks](conversation.md) has almost certainly failed to reach the person who knows the business — that is a signal to act on, not a success.

The demonstration is the point of the whole product. Everything before it exists to make it happen sooner; everything after it exists because of what it revealed.
