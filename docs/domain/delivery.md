# Real use and handover

## Going into real use

**Real use** begins when real people and real data arrive. Before that moment everything is reversible; after it, almost nothing is.

What changes:

- data has **history**, and history cannot be regenerated;
- mistakes have **victims** — someone charged twice, someone locked out, someone's private information shown to the wrong person;
- the application must keep working while it is changed.

Going into real use is a decision, taken by the customer, with the [open points](conversation.md) listed in front of them. Open points do not prevent it. Not knowing what they are does.

## Keeping the specification true

Once in real use, the pull is always toward changing the application and telling the specification later. Then never.

**Rule.** A change in real use starts as a change to the specification, exactly as it did before. The order is the same on day one and in year five: specification, then application.

A specification that has drifted from the running application is worse than none, because people still trust it. When drift is found, it is written as an [open point](conversation.md) until one side is corrected.

## Handover

At any moment, without notice and without asking permission, a customer can take **everything** — the specification, the conversation and its history, the application, and their data — and continue with a different maker, or alone.

This is a **guarantee**, not a service. It does not require the maker's cooperation, their goodwill, or their continued existence. It is not a feature that could be removed to increase retention.

**Rule.** No part of a project may depend on Supersoft continuing to exist. A project abandoned by its maker, and by Supersoft itself, must remain a working application that another maker can pick up by reading its specification.

## Why this is not negotiable

The organisations Supersoft exists for — see [Supersoft in brief](supersoft.md) — are precisely those least able to survive being locked in: no budget to be held over, no leverage, no ability to start again. A charity trapped in a tool it cannot leave has been harmed by the thing that was supposed to help it.

A maker who is easy to leave is chosen for their work. That is the only reason worth being chosen for.

## Maintenance

Most of a project's life is here: the business changes, the specification is corrected, the application follows, and the customer sees what changed and why.

An application in maintenance is not finished. It is [a project](projects.md) that has reached the state it will spend most of its life in.
