# Stories

## Story

A **story** describes one thing a person wants to do with the application, and why:

> As a *member*, I want to *find the course I started last week*, so that *I can carry on without searching*.

A story names a person, an intention, and a reason. The reason is the part most often dropped and the part that matters most: it is what lets a maker propose something better than what was asked, and what lets everyone notice later when a story no longer serves any purpose.

Stories use the [terms](specification.md) of the domain. A story that introduces a new concept is not a story yet — the concept is defined first.

## Priority

Each story carries one of:

- **essential** — the application has no purpose without it;
- **expected** — its absence would be felt as a defect;
- **later** — wanted, and explicitly not now.

**Rule.** Priority is set by the customer. The maker's contribution is the cost, stated before the priority is chosen.

## Tracking

A story is **to do**, then **in progress**, then **done**. It is done when the customer could see it working, not when the code exists.

**Rule.** What comes next is the most important story still to do. A project always knows what it is doing next, and it is one thing.
